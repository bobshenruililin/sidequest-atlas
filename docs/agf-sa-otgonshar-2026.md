## What you can actually learn (theses, not souvenirs)

These are the compounding reasons the pairing is worth a serious eight weeks. None require you to become a Mongolia specialist overnight.

1. **Return after elite school.** He wrote the return-home sentence in 2015 and then did it — banking in New York and Sydney, then ETT, cabinet, mining ministry, party, parliament. Your China CDC / Hong Kong service / law-floor confusion is a softer version of the same problem. Ask how he decided *when* to leave the bank, not how to get into HBS.

2. **Education as public infrastructure vs elite capture.** He distinguishes a **loan** fund from a **scholarship** fund, and he was listed as a beneficiary of money he never received. Your CEDARS / Youde / Tung and Ngai / stacking temptation is the Hong Kong cousin: public and donor education money as identity, not as a revolving public good. Do not dump your scholarship board on him. Do internalise the distinction.

3. **Whistleblowing from inside the governing party.** He investigated while holding senior MPP roles. That is a different risk profile from NGO advocacy. His AGF project is the legal follow-through: protection frameworks, not a press conference.

4. **Resource governance.** Mining banker → ETT → vice minister → MP on economy. Mongolia’s growth and fiscal risk sit in minerals. Your Laidlaw heat–health work is not mining law, but extractive regions, hospital load, and “who captures the surplus” are the same family of questions. Stay in your lane unless he invites you further.

5. **Unlock vs prestige knowledge.** The podcast is anti-gatekeeping: nonfiction for people who cannot buy the English hardback. HBS is the opposite scarcity machine. He built both. That tension is more interesting than either brand.

6. **Small-state craft.** Mongolia between Russia and China; Hong Kong as a dense, rules-heavy small system. If the conversation goes there, you have fieldwork instincts from the Nordics trip. Do not force Oslo as fake kinship with UWC Norway (different geography).

Pushback: your prestige hunger will want this man on a shelf next to Laidlaw, Tung and Ngai, and WEF-adjacent names. He does not need another admirer. He needs a student who can walk to the Law Library and not waste his time.

## Research help you can actually offer (HK whistleblower landscape)

AGF project: comparative whistleblower protection in selected Asian jurisdictions; legislative and institutional reforms for Mongolia.

Hong Kong is a sharp comparison **because it does not have a comprehensive whistleblower statute**. That is the point, not a failure of your briefing.

**Starting map (secondary legal commentary, not advice; re-check the ordinances themselves in the Law Library):**

- IBA / Deacons note, 6 Oct 2022: no single comprehensive ordinance; piecemeal protection for specific reporters and specific offences ([IBA](https://www.ibanet.org/best-practice-approaches-whistleblowing-hong-kong)).
- Employment Ordinance: protection where an employee gives evidence in specified employment / workplace-safety proceedings.
- Discrimination ordinances: victimisation protections.
- Prevention of Bribery Ordinance: informer identity protections; ICAC channel. Witness Protection Ordinance may apply where safety is at risk (Chambers *Anti-Corruption 2026 — Hong Kong*, accessed 25 Aug 2026).
- Securities and Futures Ordinance: civil-liability protection for reporting certain financial irregularities / resources-rule breaches.
- AML / OSCO / DTRPO / UNATMO: protected disclosures of suspected proceeds of crime; identity protections in some proceedings.
- Competition Ordinance: protection for employees who assist the Competition Commission.
- HKEX Corporate Governance Code: listed companies, whistleblowing policy on a **comply-or-explain** basis (from 1 Jan 2022 per Chambers 2026 guide). This is listing-rule governance, not a general employment right.
- Cleary Enforcement Watch (Jan 2026): ICAC expected to publish an Integrity Compliance Management System covering, among other things, whistleblower procedures for listed companies. **Whether it has been published by Sep 2026: unverified. Check ICAC / HKEX on the day you build the packet.**

**Deliverable shape (only if he says yes):** a 2–4 page annotated bibliography with ordinance names, HKEX code provision, ICAC/SFC public complaint pages, and a one-paragraph “why HK is a useful negative case.” Print it. Do not email-blast it unsolicited. You are not his lawyer and not Mongolia’s drafter.

**Do not:** take a side in Mongolian mining politics; draft his AGF paper; put his name on your scholarship applications.
