## What the ecology actually says (so you don’t twin a TED talk)

An **ecotone** is the transition between two habitats. Species composition, light, wind, and predation all change there. Some taxa thrive. **Interior specialists** often need depth: in forests, many vertebrates only peak hundreds of metres from a hard edge (Pfeifer et al., *Nature* 2017: most studied vertebrates respond to edges; core species suffer). Small patches are almost *all* edge. Gentle, old transitions ≠ a new logging road.

So: **edge is not automatically “more life.”** It is more *change*. Change is useful when two cores exist. Change is a wound when you keep cutting the core to grow perimeter.

You are not an edge *weed* ( intern, conference, award, repeat ). You are trying to be a **translator with a home range**: heat × older adults, HK as trusted interface, CHP / China CDC as aim — not a post.

## The night of bouncing

Treat it as **intermediate disturbance**, not as a personality.

Too much → nothing establishes (Youde unsent, thesis thin, twelve phenomena). A night of reading that **ends in a capture and a send** is a fire the forest is built for. A week of all-nighters is chronic wind at a new logging edge.

**Refugia:** sleep, a coherent term-time day, St John’s. Edge species that never go interior burn out. That is not toughness. That is a small patch.
