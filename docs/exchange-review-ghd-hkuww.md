## 0. Verdict up front

The question as posed — "UChicago, Columbia, LSE or Todai?" — is largely unanswerable, because **three of the four are not exchange options available to you.** The comparison set was wrong before the criteria were applied.

What the official record actually says:

### C10. "A big-name year will lift my transcript." Exchange grades never touch your GPA.

HKU's regulations are explicit: transferred credits are recorded on the transcript, but **"the results of courses completed at other institutions shall not be included in the calculation of the GPA"**, and under the Senate policy **"grades gained by students in other institutions will not appear on student transcripts."** ([BASc(GHD) regulations, GHD5](https://www4.hku.hk/pubunit/drcd/files/ugdr2023-24/Medicine/BASc\(GHD\).pdf) · [Credit Accumulation and Transfer Policy, Annex II §1.4](https://cdqa.hku.hk/doc/Credit_Accumulation_and_Transfer_Policy.pdf))

Two consequences, pulling in opposite directions:

- **Good:** exchange is GPA-risk-free. A brutal grading curve cannot damage your 4.09. Any argument of the form "X grades too hard" is void.
- **Bad:** you cannot *buy* transcript prestige either. A full year converts roughly 60 credits from graded HKU record into ungraded transfer credit — thinning the graded evidence base that MPhil and JD admissions actually read. **For a student whose strongest single number is his GPA, a full year abroad spends that asset rather than growing it.**

### C11. The mechanics you should have known before ranking anything.

Credit transfer runs on a "top-down" ECTS logic: a semester of full-time study at a host **normally converts to up to 30 HKU credits, maximum 36; a year to up to 60, maximum 72**, with a hard ceiling of **120 transferred credits** across an undergraduate degree. Approval must be obtained **before departure**, only whole courses may satisfy major requirements, and anything rejected for the major falls through to elective credit. ([Credit Accumulation and Transfer Policy §6.2, Annex II §§1.5, 2.1, 2.6–2.8](https://cdqa.hku.hk/doc/Credit_Accumulation_and_Transfer_Policy.pdf) · [Prepare to Study Abroad](https://intlaffairs.hku.hk/academic))

For calibration on how strict a home faculty can be, the Faculty of Social Sciences requires that **at least 50% of credits fulfilling a major be taken at HKU, including the capstone project**, and holds students to 24–36 HKU-equivalent credits per semester at the host. ([FSS notes for outgoing exchange students](http://www.socsc.hku.hk/students/ug/pdf/exchange/Notes%20for%20Outgoing%20Exchange%20Students_new.pdf) · [FSS outbound exchange](https://www.socsc.hku.hk/sigc/el-opportunities/sigc-options/outbound-exchange-programmes/)) **That is Social Sciences, not Medicine — do not apply it to yourself.** The equivalent HKUMed/SPH rule for BASc(GHD) is **UNVERIFIED** and is the single most important thing for you to obtain in writing.

## 2. The constraint that reorders the whole decision

Everything above is detail. This is the load-bearing finding, and it has nothing to do with rankings.

**Your capstone and every full-year exchange want the same window.**

The BASc(GHD) syllabus places `GHAD4010 Capstone in global health and development (field placement)` as follows: *"The Capstone course is normally taken from the beginning of Year 3 Summer Semester till the end of Year 4 Semester 1 (6 months)."* Its prerequisites are `GHAD1001`, `GHAD2001`, and **`GHAD3001 Global health policy` — a Year 3 course.** ([BASc(GHD) regulations and syllabus](https://www4.hku.hk/pubunit/drcd/files/ugdr2023-24/Medicine/BASc\(GHD\).pdf))

The year-by-year load is: Year 1 and Year 2 at 66 credits each, **Year 3 at 66 credits comprising a 6-credit anchoring course, 12 credits of disciplinary core, and 48 credits of electives**, Year 4 at 42 credits including the 12-credit capstone. ([same source](https://www4.hku.hk/pubunit/drcd/files/ugdr2023-24/Medicine/BASc\(GHD\).pdf))

Read those two facts together:

- **Year 3 is 48/66 credits of elective space.** It is, structurally, the year the curriculum built to absorb an exchange. A single semester there transfers at up to 30 credits against 48 credits of elective room — the cleanest possible fit, with no major-requirement fight.
- **A full year in Year 3 breaks the capstone chain.** You would be away for `GHAD3001`, which is the prerequisite for `GHAD4010`. Miss it and the capstone cannot start in Year 3 Summer as designed; it slides into Year 4 and collides with Year 4 coursework and graduation timing.
- **Every full-year option forces exactly that.** LSE is full-year-only and requires two completed years. Todai requires two completed years. Oxford Worldwide Plus is full-year and prefers second-years going in third year. All three land on Year 3. ([HKUWW 2026-27 list](https://intlaffairs.hku.hk/backend/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/HKUWW2627_20260311.pdf) · [Worldwide Plus Visiting](https://intlaffairs.hku.hk/worldwide-plus-visiting))

And the capstone is not a generic requirement. It is *the* vehicle for the China CDC ambition: a compulsory 12-credit, 4–6 month field placement facilitated by the School of Public Health, in organisations including UN agencies, development banks, NGOs and consultancies. ([Capstone overseas field placement](https://sph.hku.hk/en/Bachelor-of-Arts-and-Sciences-in-Global-Health-and-Development/Curriculum/Capstone-Overseas-Field-Placement) · [HKUMed BASc(GHD) deck](https://www.med.hku.hk/en/students/ug/-/media/F47EE79F2C724E86B5CFF47814185813.ashx))

**So the trade is explicit: a full year at LSE, Todai or Oxford is purchased with the China CDC placement window.** Your own read-back profile lists that placement as the degree-level aim and the thing that makes the T&N commitment narrative concrete rather than rhetorical. Trading it for a brand-name year is the single largest unforced error available to you right now.

**Note also** that the capstone is officially described as an *overseas* placement, and it is *arranged by HKU*. Whether a mainland China posting satisfies "overseas", and whether China CDC is an approvable host, are **UNVERIFIED**. If the answer is no, the entire China CDC plan needs rebuilding — and that is a more urgent question than any exchange destination.

### The one-slot rule, stated properly

HKUWW eligibility requires that you have **"Not joined any previous semester exchange programmes or special visiting programmes organised by IAO, faculties or departments."** ([HKUWW programme page](https://intlaffairs.hku.hk/hku-worldwide-student-exchange))

You get **one**. Also relevant: non-final-year only, students in their second-to-last or last semester may not apply, and **Year 2+ students are given higher placement priority than Year 1 students**. That last clause is a straightforward argument against applying early.

What does *not* appear to consume the slot — because HKU's own Horizons taxonomy separates "Exchange/Visiting" (HKUWW plus faculty-level exchange) from "Short-term Study/Study Trip" ([HKU Horizons overview](https://tl.hku.hk/horizons/overview/)):

- **LINK** — Leadership and Innovation Network for Knowledge: 12 academic credits plus a 3-week immersion in Shanghai or Ho Chi Minh City, open to all faculties and years, HK$5,000 subsidy. ([LINK](https://tl.hku.hk/link/)) Twelve credits and Shanghai, for three weeks and no slot cost.
- **China Vision** and its funding schemes — the **Non-means-tested Mainland Experience Scheme** (once per undergraduate, minimum 3 days, mainland, organised by an HKU faculty/department/unit, open to local *and* non-local students), the **GHMUA Student Interflow Scheme**, and the **GBA learning-activities subsidy**. ([China Vision](https://www.mainlandaffairs.hku.hk/chinavision) · [Non-means-tested MES](https://www.chinavision.hku.hk/non-means-tested-mainland-experienc) · [GHMUA Interflow](https://www.chinavision.hku.hk/ghmua-student-interflow-scheme))
- **Summer sessions** under Recognized Programmes — including **PKU Summer School International**, **Fudan International Summer Session**, **SJTU Global Summer School**, and **UTokyo Global Unit Courses at a 15% discount**. ([Recognized Programmes](https://intlaffairs.hku.hk/recognized-programmes))

**This is the whole one-slot argument, and it is decisive.** Mainland China access is cheap, repeatable, subsidised, and available through at least four separate non-HKUWW channels — before you even count the **compulsory Mainland exchange that Tung & Ngai itself imposes** (`docs/tung-ngai-bob-profile.md`). HKUWW is the *only* instrument that buys you a subsidised full semester at a non-Chinese institution.

**Spending your single HKUWW slot on PKU is therefore double-counting an asset you are already going to receive twice over.** It is the "twinning" failure the operator doctrine names: it feels like commitment, but it purchases nothing you don't already own, while retiring the one thing you can't get elsewhere. If you want Beijing, take PKU Summer School and keep the slot.

**Caution:** the eligibility wording — "special visiting programmes organised by IAO, faculties or departments" — is broad. LINK is run by Teaching and Learning rather than IAO and is categorised as short-term study, so it should be safe, but **get IAO to confirm in writing before you enrol in anything**. The downside of being wrong is losing the slot entirely.

## 4. Oxford Worldwide Plus, priced

You called it a false bargain. It is worse than you framed it, and for a reason you did not name.

**What it costs.** Worldwide Plus Visiting is explicitly not an exchange: "students will need to pay full academic tuition fees." At **St Peter's College**, 2026/27 reference figures are £41,390 for Economics, History, Philosophy, Politics or PPE (£43,510 for Geography), plus £8,806 accommodation on a 37-week lease. At **Pembroke College**, £47,648 tuition plus £7,666 accommodation plus £1,378 meal plan — a stated total of **£56,692**. Airfares are additional. Against that, the HKU Worldwide Visiting Scholarship is **HK$100,000 for a full-year programme**. ([Worldwide Plus Visiting](https://intlaffairs.hku.hk/worldwide-plus-visiting))

Compare the counterfactual: on an HKUWW *exchange* you pay HKU's composition fee and no host tuition. HKU's 2026-27 composition fee is HK$47,000 for local students and, for non-local students in **STEM faculties — which includes the Faculty of Medicine, and therefore GHD** — HK$249,000. ([HKU composition fee 2026-27](https://handbook.hku.hk/ug/full-time-2026-27/fees-awards-and-financial-assistance/composition-fee.html) · [HKU tuition and living expenses](https://www.admissions.hku.hk/fees-and-scholarships/fees))

**What it does not buy.** The St Peter's subject list is Archaeology & Anthropology, Economics, English, History, History of Art, Management, Modern Languages, Philosophy, Politics, PPE, Theology, Mathematics, Statistics, Geography, Music, Neuroscience, Biology, Earth Sciences, Engineering Science, Physics. ([Worldwide Plus Visiting](https://intlaffairs.hku.hk/worldwide-plus-visiting)) **No medicine, no public health, no global health, no epidemiology.** You would buy an Oxford *Economics or Geography* year.

**The three costs you were not counting:**

1. It is **full-year and pitched at third-years** — so it takes the capstone window (§2).
2. It is a **"special visiting programme organised by IAO"**, so on the plain wording it **burns the HKUWW slot** as well. One instrument, both assets.
3. Eligibility requires **CGPA 3.7 or above**. You clear that at 4.09 — which is the point: you are qualified enough for this to be a genuine temptation rather than a fantasy, and that is exactly when it is dangerous.

**Verdict: correctly identified as a false bargain, for stronger reasons than prestige-scepticism.** It is roughly £50,000–57,000 against a HK$100,000 scholarship, for coursework outside your field, paid for with both the capstone window and the exchange slot. If you want Oxford on the CV at a defensible price, the Recognized Programmes list carries **Exeter College Summer Programme** and **Lady Margaret Hall LMH Summer** — fee-paying, short, and slot-free. ([Recognized Programmes](https://intlaffairs.hku.hk/recognized-programmes))

## 5. On the "rank versus tool" question you actually asked

You asked whether Chicago is the wrong faculty for a GHD student who thinks he is buying a public-health year. Two layers.

**The narrow answer is yes, on the merits.** UChicago has **no undergraduate public health major.** Its undergraduate routes into the field are a **Health and Society minor**, a **Biological Sciences major with an Interdisciplinary Focus in Global and Public Health**, and a **Public Policy major with a Health Policy area of specialisation**. ([Health and Society, UChicago catalog](http://collegecatalog.uchicago.edu/thecollege/healthandsociety/) · [Biological Sciences catalog](http://uchicago-public.courseleaf.com/thecollege/biologicalsciences/) · [Public Policy catalog](http://uchicago-public.courseleaf.com/thecollege/publicpolicystudies/)) The Department of Public Health Sciences teaches at graduate level — `PBHS 30910 Epidemiology` sits in the 30000 series, and its concentrations in biostatistics, epidemiology and health services research are built around a Student Advisory Committee and research workshops. ([PBHS core curriculum](https://pbhs.uchicago.edu/program/core-curriculum) · [PBHS major area of concentration](https://pbhs.uchicago.edu/page/major-area-concentration)) A visiting undergraduate at Chicago buys the Core and economics. Your instinct was right.

**But the broader answer is that you framed the whole question one tier too high.** You compared Chicago against "Mailman / LSHTM / SPH". Those are not comparable objects:

- **LSHTM is a postgraduate-only institution.** ([LSHTM postgraduate prospectus 2026-27](https://www.lshtm.ac.uk/files/pg_prospectus.pdf))
- **Mailman is the graduate school of public health of Columbia**, offering MPH, MS, MHA, PhD and DrPH, with undergraduate access only through 4+1 BA/MPH tracks reserved for Columbia College, Columbia GS, Barnard and Vassar students. ([Mailman FAQ](https://www.publichealth.columbia.edu/about-us/frequently-asked-questions) · [Mailman degree programs](https://www.publichealth.columbia.edu/academics/degrees))

**There is no elite-SPH undergraduate year to buy, anywhere.** The world's best public health schools do not teach undergraduates. So the correct target for an exchange semester is not "the best public health school I can reach" — that set is empty — but **"the best undergraduate population-health teaching I can reach that transfers cleanly."** By that criterion the list reorders completely, and UBC wins.

The good news, which you should internalise rather than resent: **the elite-SPH tier is a postgraduate door, and it is still fully open.** Mailman runs a **JD/MPH** with Columbia Law and an **MPA/MPH** with SIPA ([Mailman degree programs](https://www.publichealth.columbia.edu/become-student/degree-programs)) — which is your "JD after UG as regulatory tool" and your health-policy ambition in a single programme. LSHTM's MSc and DrPH are open to you on the strength of a GHD degree. Nothing about your exchange choice gates any of that. What *does* gate it is the graded HKU transcript and the letters — which is precisely the argument for a semester rather than a year (§C10).
