# Fukuda career sharing — questions to choose from

**Event (operator-stated, not independently posted here):** Sharing on a career in global health and development  
**Speaker:** Professor Keiji Fukuda, Honorary Professor, School of Public Health  
**When:** Friday 28 Aug 2026, 14:30–16:30  
**Where:** LE2, Library Extension, LG1/F, HKU Main Campus  

**Who he is (verified against SPH bio + HKU Honorary University Fellowship citation, accessed 2026-08-16):** Physician-epidemiologist. Oberlin BA, Vermont MD, Berkeley MPH, CDC Epidemic Intelligence Service. ~20 years CDC (Chief of Epidemiology, Influenza Branch). WHO 2005–2016 (Global Influenza Programme; Assistant Director-General for Health Security; AMR special adviser). **He introduced BASc(GHD) as SPH Director (2017–2021).** 1997 H5N1 field lead in Hong Kong; SARS 2003 in Hong Kong, Beijing, Shanghai. COVID advisory roles to HKSAR. Now Honorary Professor; China Medical Board and RIGHT Foundation boards; Ending Pandemics advisory board.

You are in the degree he built. Do not ask him how to get into WHO. Ask him what GHD is *for*, and what a non-physician should do in Year 2.

---

## How to use the room

You will get **one**, maybe two, questions. Pick from the starred set. Have one backup. Do not recap his CV. Do not ask for a placement.

**One-line ID if you need it:** “I’m a Year 2 GHD student. I work on heat and older adults, and I want to work at the Hong Kong–Mainland public-health interface.”

Do **not** lead with birthplace, scholar titles, or HA data claims. Do **not** ask him to get you China CDC, WHO, or CMB. A GHD student has already been placed at China CDC Shanghai and at CMB Bangkok — those are programme facts, not personal favours.

---

## If you only get one shot — pick two of these three

**A1.** You introduced GHD as a multi-sectoral undergraduate degree, not a junior MPH and not a pre-med. I will not hold a medical licence. In a Hong Kong CHP or IHR-facing room, what work can a GHD graduate actually own in the first five years — and what should we stop pretending we can do?

**B1.** Official WHO WPRO internships ask for age ≥20 *and* three completed university years, and the copy still reads like a postgraduate programme. For a GHD student, is a year inside a **national** health-security shop (CHP, a Mainland CDC, an IHR focal point) more load-bearing than a WHO intern badge — and when, if ever, does the badge become worth it?

**C1.** You have described the 2016 UN heads-of-state AMR meeting as your greatest professional achievement, not H1N1. If a GHD student has to choose, should we train for the famous emergency or for the unsexy diplomacy that moves a technical problem onto a national agenda?

## Full menu (choose; do not spray)

### A. The degree he invented

**A1.** ★ GHD without an MD — what can we own in a CHP/IHR room in the first five years?  
**A2.** GHD names climate, ageing, urbanisation, *and* infectious threats. Your own career is health security and influenza. Was the degree built for people like you, or for people who will never be EIS officers? What graduate do you *not* want GHD to produce?  
**A3.** You combined epidemiological and laboratory data to improve US influenza surveillance. If a GHD student can add only one thing in Year 2 — computation, a Mainland language used in the field, or another semester of epidemiology — which one actually changes employability?

### B. WHO, nations, and badges

**B1.** ★ National IHR/CHP year vs WHO intern badge.  
**B2.** You managed WHO’s H1N1 response — the first major test of the IHR. From the inside, what competence is built only in a **ministry or CDC**, and what is built only in Geneva/Manila?  
**B3.** PIP Framework is virus sharing versus benefit sharing. For a student aiming at the Hong Kong–Mainland interface, is the analogous problem **data and trust** (who shares surveillance, who gets the tools), and where should we practise that before we talk about WHO?

### C. Hong Kong, China, the seam

**C1.** ★ AMR diplomacy vs famous emergency.  
**C2.** You led CDC teams in Hong Kong in 1997 and worked Hong Kong, Beijing, and Shanghai in 2003. When cross-boundary outbreak work actually functions, what is the scarce skill — laboratory, epidemiology, or the ability to make two systems trust each other’s numbers?  
**C3.** GHD already documents a China CDC Shanghai capstone and a CMB Bangkok placement. You sit on the China Medical Board. What can a **six-month undergraduate** contribute there that is not a burden on the host — and what preparation do students skip?

### D. Trust, uncertainty, naming

**D1.** In 2010 you said WHO did not convey the uncertainty of H1N1, and that was read as non-transparency. For someone whose thesis is public trust at a seam, what is the practical drill — how do you speak when the severity scale and the geographic scale disagree?  
**D2.** Your 2015 *Science* note argued “naming diseases: first do no harm.” How should a Hong Kong–Mainland practitioner talk about origin, stigma, and accuracy without performing either nationalism or denial?

### E. Climate-health vs the career you actually lived

**E1.** Heat and ageing are in the GHD brochure. Your publications and posts are influenza, SARS, AMR. Is climate-health a real hiring lane in Asian public health, or a curriculum layer until the next pathogen? If it is real, where does it sit relative to hospital surge and surveillance — not relative to conferences?  
**E2.** Dual-season hospital pressure (heat and respiratory) looks like one operations problem. Is that a serious way to join health security without becoming an influenza specialist?

### F. How to spend Year 2 (the only operational question)

**F1.** You almost went to arts / National Geographic, then took a long travelling gap, then medicine, EIS, WHO. For a GHD Year 2 with one winter immersion and one summer already committed, what should we **refuse** this year so the capstone is not a stamp collection?  
**F2.** After you left the SPH directorship, what do you wish the first GHD cohorts had done more of, and less of, while they were still undergraduates?
