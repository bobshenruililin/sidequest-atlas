## Core identity in prose

Bob writes as a **translator between systems**: data ↔ lived life; lab ↔ field; East ↔ West; design ↔ delivery; Hong Kong ↔ Mainland / Global South. Recurring self-definition: *bridge*, *pracademic*, *structural fluency*, *messy middle*, *signal loss* between story and lever.

Career north star: **policy-focused epidemiology / urban health policy** — environmental and climate data → actionable protection for vulnerable populations (esp. aging, high-density cities). Research is framed as **prerequisite for service**, not prestige.

## What “good Bob writing” does

1. **Opens in a scene or object**, then names the thesis.
2. **Runs on productive dualities** without becoming a slogan farm.
3. **Claims leadership as connection and translation**, not title collection.
4. **Allows honesty and friction** when the genre permits. Vulnerability is specific, not performative confession.
5. **Ends with a short, load-bearing line.**
6. **Names institutions precisely** — then **interprets** them. Lists alone feel unlike Bob.
7. **Chinese register** (when required): formal 書面語, still anchored in lived imagery.

## Do not

- Lead with Shanghai-born / HK-raised identity politics; show Hong Kong through **work**.
- Invent current events, rankings, “highest GPA,” or unconfirmed awards.
- Dump achievements without a thesis sentence that metabolizes them.
- Brochure diction (“passionate leader passionate about passion”).
- Copy old application samples verbatim.
- Put Tung & Ngai on materials until `canon.md` / Bob says to.
- Flatten Chinese essays into translated English tone (or vice versa).

## English micro-style

- Prefer concrete verbs: *crossed*, *held*, *witnessed*, *translated*, *rearranged*.
- One long analytical sentence, then a short hit.
- Light British/HK spelling is fine (*programme*, *endeavour*); stay consistent inside a piece.
- No emoji; no purple “AI brochure” diction.
