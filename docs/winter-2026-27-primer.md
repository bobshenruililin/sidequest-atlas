## 0. How to read this

IAO’s Recognised Programmes page is **a reference list of programmes HKU students have previously attended**, last updated **21 May 2026**. It is not an exchange catalogue and not a promise of credit. Short-term programmes are, unless otherwise noted, **not available on an exchange basis**: you apply to the host and you pay the host.

LINK is not on that list. LINK is an **HKU 12-credit Horizons course** with a three-week immersion. Comparing LINK-S to Yonsei is comparing a home-course field component to a host winter school. Different products. The question is which *life* you want in that gap, not which logo looks busier on a CV.

**How numbers are labelled**

- **Published / dated:** copied from an official page with access date 22 Aug 2026 unless noted.
- **Arithmetic:** addition of published subsidy rows. Not a quote.
- **UNVERIFIED:** not on an official page we fetched, internally inconsistent, or the official page is silent / stale.
- **Proxy:** last published edition used as *shape* for an unpublished 2027 cycle. Never treat a proxy as a booking.

Stars (★) are **operator scores** on five axes: experience, meaning, memory, calendar-fit, NEWDAY-likeness. They are not host ratings. A 2★ memory in a city you have never seen can still beat a 4★ course in a city you already know, depending on what you are actually trying to maximise.

**Subagents used (2026-08-22):** parallel crawls of Europe, Korea/Japan, Americas/China/Oceania, and NEWDAY/city texture, plus direct fetches of official HTML. This document is the synthesis. Host sites change without notice. Re-read anything past `reverifyAfter` in `.tmp/winter-primer/claims.jsonl`.

## 1. Three winters

There are winters you inherit and winters you buy.

The inherited one is a kitchen in Shanghai in late December. The extractor fan. The wet cold that never quite becomes snow. A grandparent who will not say how long the visit is allowed to last. You already know this city in its bones. Going back is not discovery. It is maintenance of a relationship that does not care about your transcript.

The bought one, last summer, was a garden in Lillehammer where, if the weather permitted, you sat talking around a campfire in light that refused to die. Nansen priced that at **11,000 NOK** for a double room, food and lodging included, **nine days**, travel extra. You loved it. That is a data point, not a personality quiz. It says you want a small room, a real argument, woods, and people from the other side of a map who are not performing cosmopolitanism.

The third winter is the one IAO listed: SKY campuses that turn December into a credit mill; European January programmes that begin after your second semester has already started; a Chilean classical-liberal seminar that would send you to the Andes six months before Laidlaw already sends you toward the Andes; a Fudan classroom three metro stops from the grandparents you could have been eating with instead.

You asked for experience, meaning, and memory. Those are not the same currency.

- **Experience** is what the days are *made of*: lectures, projects, walking, cold, other people.
- **Meaning** is whether the days metabolise into a thesis you can still use in April.
- **Memory** is what will still be specific in ten years: a smell, a dark street, a person, a room.

NEWDAY scored high on all three because the form did the work: residential, dialogic, small, in a landscape that was itself an argument. Winter 2026/27 cannot repurchase that form. Nansen has **no winter equivalent**. NEWDAY 2027 is **unpublished**. The honest move is to stop shopping for Lillehammer in January and choose among the winters that actually exist.

## 9. Cities as memory machines

Climate here is **character**, not a forecast. No invented hourly weather. No invented events.

**Shanghai in the gap.** A humid-subtropical city at its greyest. Indoor life. Family time that does not photograph well and does not need to. You will not “see Shanghai.” You will be from it. The memory, if you protect it, is intergenerational rather than panoramic. LINK either serves that or it crowds it — that is a calendar you design, not a programme feature.

**Seoul in late December.** Early sunset, dry cold, campus winter schools running on a skeleton of local students. Neon and convenience stores and a very professional international office. First-Korea memory is cheap to romanticise and actually delivered by buses, fried chicken at 11pm, and a commencement you did not need. It is a real place. It is also the most replaceable “world I haven’t seen” on this list, because SKY summer schools will sell you the same campus in July.

**Vilnius in early January.** Baltic winter: little daylight, a baroque old town that reads as Europe-without-the-brochure-density of Paris. A language course that forces humility. High memory per euro if you like being slightly lost. Low GHD.

**Berlin in early January.** You have seen the plenary version. The January version is residential-neighbourhood dark, museums without queues, a seminar on refugees in a city that has had to mean that word. Return can be deeper than first-ness. It can also be a rerun. Only you know which.

**Tübingen in January.** A Neckar university town. Hostel breakfasts. Host families who have done this for years. Sustainability as cobbles and recycling rules. This is the European winter that would have understood NEWDAY. The calendar does not.

**Lille in January.** A Flemish-French industrial capital with a Eurostar-shaped geography (Bruges, Reims on the package). Hotel twins. Buddy system. Easy, dense, social. Memory of *being well taken care of*, which is not nothing and not Nansen.

**Santiago in January.** Southern summer, Andes on the skyline, Providencia hotel, campus bus to UANDES. Heat, not snow. A political-economy classroom with a point of view. Valparaíso is a UNESCO slope of houses. Fine. Wrong year relative to LiA, wrong dates relative to Sem 2.

**Melbourne / Burwood in January.** Southern summer, surf day in the fee, Australian Studies unit. A first-Australia memory with a packaged shape. Overruns.

**Blacksburg, Virginia, in January.** A campus town in the mountains after the undergraduates have gone home. Empty and specific. Only makes sense as a *course*, not as tourism. Pathway not ready.

**Lillehammer, for calibration.** Long light, woods, campfire *if weather permits*, two Nobel houses, dialogue groups. Summer, not winter. The gold standard remains in another season. Keep NEWDAY 2027 as a watch, not a winter plan. Historically 2026 admissions opened 20 January — a **pattern**, not a 2027 date.

## 10. Money without inventing

No household figures. No FX conversions. Original currency only.

**LINK cash in.** HK$5,000 to each student. HK$10,000 MicroBit after a pass, for high achievers (not guaranteed). Major costs “covered” per the eligibility page — still budget flights/insurance as **UNVERIFIED** extras.

**SSE / SSEBR (short-term, AY2026-27 page).** Eligible activity window **1 Sep 2026 – 31 Aug 2027**. Apply within **1 month of completion**. SSE: **once** per UG, irrespective of amount; means-tested (SFO or CSSA). Asia destination row **HK$3,000**; 14–28 days duration **HK$6,000**. SSEBR Asia row **excludes the Chinese Mainland, Taiwan and Macao**. Non-means-tested SSEBR: **once**, lifetime cap **HK$18,000**. Same destination cannot be paid twice across the schemes. University **adjusts downward** if other subsidy exists — MicroBit HK$5,000 is therefore not a clean add-on to a full government ceiling.

**Do not spend SSE on winter.** The over-180-days SSE duration band is **HK$45,000** before destination. That is the year-long exchange. Keep the once.

**Non-means-tested MES.** Mainland only, **≥3 days**, **once** per UG, reimbursement. Worked example on the China Vision page: 95 days → $900 + $7,500 = $8,400. For a 14–28 day Mainland activity the duration piece is **$1,800**, plus destination **$900**, arithmetic ceiling **$2,700**, then downward adjustment. **Catch:** the live China Vision page still states an eligible period **1 Sep 2025 – 31 Aug 2026**. Confirm the **2026-27** MES window before treating LINK-S as eligible. Local *and* non-local students are eligible on that page — unlike SSE.

**IAO short-term scholarships.** In-person, ≥2 weeks, GPA ≥2.0. **Largest-single-award** rule across the undergraduate career. Scale “as of AY 2025-26”: Zone 4 (Japan/Korea) **HK$6,000**; Zone 5 (Mainland outside Guangdong) **HK$5,000**; Zone 1 (Europe/US/Chile) **HK$14,000**; Zone 2 (Australia) **HK$13,000**. Winter 2026/27 registration deadline **not posted**. Last updated **9 Sep 2025**.

**T&N HK$80k.** Shared compulsory-Mainland + optional-international cap. **Not assumed** to fund LINK. Compulsory-Mainland satisfaction via LINK: **UNVERIFIED**.
