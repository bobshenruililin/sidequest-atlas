# Why join a leadership cohort in transition — prep sheet

**For:** Tung & Ngai (or any serious scholar community)  
**Do not recite thinkers in interview.** Use as backstage.

---

## The answer skeleton (Kissinger → your verbs)

In periods when institutions and values are contested, leadership means **diagnosis**: what sustains well-being, what decays, what to preserve/adapt/discard, which temptations to refuse, whether sacrifice is bearable.

**I am joining a cohort because I need shared diagnostic practice** — peers who will challenge whether my research is useful, whether my Mainland engagement is serious, and whether I returned anything — not because I need another title.

---

---

## Spoken version (~45–60s, no names)

> I’m applying in a time when Hong Kong’s role and China’s development are both contested — and when public-health systems have to work across boundaries that pathogens and heat do not respect. What I need is not only funding, but a community that forces diagnostic questions: what should be preserved, adapted, or discarded in how we prepare for harm; which opportunities are tempting but empty; and whether I’m willing to do the slow work that returns capacity to Hong Kong. That’s why a multi-year scholar community matters to me more than a one-off award.

---

## Stress-test yourself before saying it

- Can you name one inheritance to preserve and one temptation to reject?  
- Can you name one sacrifice that is *yours*, not someone else’s?  
- Would this answer still stand if you already had enough money?
